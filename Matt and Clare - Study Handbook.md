## What This Study Is

This is a **complete KJV Bible study** built on the psychological and mythological framework of Jordan Peterson's *Biblical Series* lectures. It covers all **66 books** of the Bible across **42 study units**, designed specifically for the two of you to explore together as a couple building toward a shared life.

The approach treats Biblical narratives not as mere historical records but as **deeply evolved structures of meaning** — humanity's attempt to articulate the patterns of consciousness, morality, sacrifice, and redemption that underlie human experience.

## Core Concepts Dictionary

### Chaos vs. Order
The fundamental duality. Chaos is the unknown, the unstructured, the potential — symbolized by water, darkness, the dragon. Order is the known, the structured, the predictable — symbolized by walls, law, the father. Healthy life requires navigating between too much chaos (overwhelm) and too much order (tyranny).

### The Logos (Word/Reason)
The divine ordering principle — "In the beginning was the Word." Logos represents truthful speech and conscious attention as the force that transforms chaos into habitable order. Speaking truth is participating in the creative act of God.

### The Shadow (Jung)
The dark, rejected, unconscious part of the personality. Contains both destructive potential and untapped creative energy. Integration of the shadow — acknowledging your capacity for evil — is essential for moral development. Cain represents the unintegrated shadow.

### Sacrifice
The foundational human discovery: you can give up something of value now for something better in the future. Evolves from animal sacrifice → ritual sacrifice → psychological sacrifice (giving up lesser pleasures for greater meaning). Abel's accepted offering vs. Cain's rejected one.

### Individuation (Jung)
The process of becoming your authentic self by integrating all parts of the psyche — conscious and unconscious, light and shadow, masculine and feminine. Abraham leaving his father's house is the archetype of individuation.

### The Call to Adventure (Campbell)
The divine summons to leave the known world and venture into the unknown. Requires sacrificing security for meaning. Abraham's call is the foundational pattern. Refusing the call leads to stagnation and resentment.

### Archetype
A universal pattern of human experience embedded in the collective unconscious. The Hero, the Great Mother, the Wise Old Man, the Trickster, the Shadow — these appear across all cultures because they represent fundamental structures of consciousness.

### Covenant
A mutual binding commitment between the divine principle and the individual. Not a one-sided demand but a reciprocal relationship requiring faithful action from both parties. The foundation of marriage, community, and civilization.

### The Fall
The emergence of self-consciousness — knowing good and evil, becoming aware of mortality and vulnerability. Not merely punishment but the necessary price of becoming fully human. Shame, suffering, and death enter through awareness.

### Redemption
The possibility of restoration after the Fall — not return to innocence but the achievement of a higher integration through conscious moral effort. Joseph's story is the Genesis archetype: betrayal → suffering → integrity → redemption.

## How to Use This Study

1. **Read the assigned KJV passages together** (or individually, then discuss).
2. **Read the unit summary** to orient yourselves in the psychological themes.
3. **Discuss the couple's questions** — these are designed to connect the ancient text to your lived experience together.
4. **No fixed timeline** — move at your own pace. Some units may take one session, others may stretch across several weeks.
5. **Journal if you want** — writing down your reflections deepens the experience.
6. **Watch Peterson's lectures** for Units 1-15 if you want the full experience (YouTube: "Jordan Peterson Biblical Series").
